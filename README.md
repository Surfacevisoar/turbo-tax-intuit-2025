# TurboTax (Intuit)

![TurboTax headquarters](assets/cover.jpg)

*The Intuit campus in Mountain View, California, where TurboTax has been built and maintained for decades.*

TurboTax is the consumer tax-preparation software line published by Intuit. It has been around since the mid-1980s, originally under the name MacInTax before being rebranded. Today, when people search for "turbo tax intuit," they usually mean the desktop application, the web version, or simply want to confirm that TurboTax is indeed an Intuit product — which it is.

This repository is a small reference note about the product itself, not an official support page. If you landed here after googling "turbo tax intuit," you probably want to know what TurboTax is, which editions exist, and how it relates to Intuit's other products.

## What TurboTax Actually Is

TurboTax is a guided tax preparation program. You answer questions about your income, deductions, and life events, and the software fills in the appropriate tax forms for you. It is not a substitute for professional advice, but it is designed so that a person with no accounting background can complete a return.

The product line splits into a few editions:

- **TurboTax Free Edition** — for simple returns (W-2 income only, no itemizing).
- **TurboTax Deluxe** — for homeowners and people who itemize deductions.
- **TurboTax Premier** — for investors and people with rental property.
- **TurboTax Self-Employed** — for freelancers, contractors, and small business owners.

Each edition adds more schedules and guidance. The desktop download version and the online version are separate products with slightly different pricing and features, and they are not interchangeable — a return started in one cannot be moved to the other.

## Why "TurboTax Intuit" Is a Common Search

People type "turbo tax intuit" for a handful of reasons:

1. **They are not sure who makes it.** Intuit is the parent company, and the connection is not obvious from the product name alone.
2. **They want the official site** and are trying to avoid lookalike domains or third-party resellers.
3. **They are comparing Intuit's own products** — TurboTax, QuickBooks, and Mint are all Intuit brands, and it is easy to mix up which one does what.
4. **They are looking for account help** and know Intuit handles billing and login for all its services.

If you already have an Intuit account, the same credentials work across TurboTax, QuickBooks, and Mint. That single sign-on is often why people end up searching for the parent company name in the first place.

## Common Gotchas

- **The free edition is genuinely free only for simple returns.** If you have freelance income, rental property, or itemized deductions, the software will push you toward a paid tier.
- **Desktop and online are different products.** The CD/download version is a one-time purchase for a single tax year. The online version is a subscription-style flow where you pay when you file.
- **Your data does not auto-carry between years.** Each year's return is a separate file. You can import last year's data from the prior year's software, but you have to initiate that manually.
- **State returns are separate.** Federal and state preparation are priced independently, and the state return is an add-on in most editions.
